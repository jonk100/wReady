Jenny looked back and laughed confidently.

"The house is not going to tip over." 

She attempted to ease a few fears, but failed.

Differing reports had been weaving their way through the small crowd all night. Ricky heard a whisper when Dale told Carla. Little Arty saw Gary gesturing in a sort of way that made it look like he thought the house would tip over. Kathy was sure of it. She urged everybody to be ready - the house would tip over, and probably tonight.

Most everyone was enjoying another wonderful Thanksgiving, despite the rumors. They filled their bellies with food and beer, watched mostly bad football, and caught up. The age-old tradition of asking the kids about their schooling was alive and well. 

If it were only Jenny, they might have been more concerned. But Jenny's son, Jon, was the kind of young man who wouldn't say he was sure of something unless he was sure of it - and Jon was absolutely sure that the house wouldn't tip over. He wandered around, answering questions about his schooling like a good boy, and assuring the guests that they didnt need to worry about the house. It wasnt going to tip over.

To Jenny, it seemed like her and Jon were the only sane people in the house. To Jon, it seemed like he and Jenny were the only reasonable man and woman left on the planet. When Steve pointed out that a couple picture frames were crooked, they laughed it off and said they were probably always like that. When the torn up tennis balls all rolled into the same corner, they just thought someone must have been playing with the dog, Layla. 

As the walls began to cave in and the jovial holiday laughter shifted to a symphony of shrieks of horror, Jon came to the realization that the house was tipping over. Acting quickly, he gathered everyone together, shielded them as best he could from falling rubbel, and led them out onto the front driveway. 

Everyone hugged each other, cried tears of joy, clapped for Jon, and maybe even chanted his name. But he couldnt find Layla! Was she still stuck inside? 

As they frantically searched the mounds of debris, somebody saw her.

"There she is!" they exclaimed. 

She was laying in the front yard, unharmed.

"Awe... Good girl," they muttered.

The End
