# The Press: Amplification and Manufacture

The media's role in transforming private statements into public scandals, creating the appearance of popular outrage that justifies institutional action.

## Key Elements

### Context Stripping
- **Editor selection:** Pull quotes chosen for maximum controversy, not accuracy
- **Headline fabrication:** Sensationalism trumps factual reporting
- **Geographic amplification:** Local stories become national scandals through reprinting

### Outrage Supply Chain
- **Letters to editor:** Coordinated campaigns create appearance of spontaneous response
- **Moral majority illusion:** Small but vocal opposition presented as mainstream consensus
- **International replication:** UK stories picked up by US Bible Belt and vice versa

### Institutional Complicity
- **Press conferences:** Turn conversations into trials (Chicago press conference)
- **Deadline pressure:** Forces reporting before context or verification possible
- **Access journalism:** Reporters protect sources who provide exclusive information

## Irony and Impact

- The press believes it's serving public interest while manufacturing public opinion
- Individual journalists may be ethical, but the system incentivizes sensationalism
- The Beatles' actual words matter less than how they're framed and distributed
