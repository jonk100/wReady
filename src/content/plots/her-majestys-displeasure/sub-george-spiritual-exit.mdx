# George: The Spiritual Exit Ramp

George Harrison's journey from under-credited third force to spiritual seeker, using sitar music and Eastern philosophy as both genuine evolution and strategic distance from the band.

## Key Elements

### Sitar Path Seeded
- **Nigel's nudge:** The operation subtly encourages George's interest in Indian music
- **Ravi Shankar apprenticeship:** George's musical evolution becomes spiritual journey
- **"Maya" concept:** George adopts philosophical framework for dismissing band identity

### Growing Withdrawal
- **Musical contributions:** Increasingly sophisticated but unacknowledged within band
- **Private victory:** George solves musical problems alone, highlighting band dysfunction
- **India journey:** Spiritual seeking becomes geographic and emotional distance

### First Explicit Fracture
- **Twickenham exit:** George's "I'm leaving" is the band's first explicit breakup moment
- **Artistic recognition:** George needs respect the band structure cannot provide
- **Post-band resolution:** Garden, simplicity, sitar as ordinary object rather than spiritual quest

## Dual Meaning

- **Genuine evolution:** George's spiritual and musical growth is authentic
- **Strategic exit:** The spiritual journey provides legitimate reason to distance himself
- **Artistic independence:** George's songwriting evolution requires freedom from band constraints

## Relationship to Other Plots

- **Exploited by:** The Crown's Covert War (A-plot) through cultural manipulation
- **Demonstrates:** Beatles Creative Fracture (E-subplot) through explicit departure
- **Contrasts with:** Paul's need for structure and John's political awakening
